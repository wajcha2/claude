## [method] a1b29ba: hwv_fast.py + sweep_fast.py (30-300x faster, bit-identical matrices)
* Idea 1 (Cauchy-Binet): a column determinant det(V_{s_c} g[:, :|c|]) equals sum_I det g[I, :|c|] * det V[s_c, I] over
  |c|-subsets I of [n]. In the tensor network every column op (batch x r^|c|) becomes two small ops joined by a bond
  of size C(n,|c|) <= 6 (a length-4 column becomes det(g) * det(V)). The path optimiser can then route the
  contraction through Lambda^{|c|} C^4 instead of r^|c| word indices.
* Idea 2: flop-minimising path (opt_einsum RandomGreedy, 128 tries) instead of the size-greedy order.
* Idea 3: exact float64 BLAS. All tensors hold residues 0..p-1 as float64; products are < p^2 < 2^38, so dot
  products of <= 2^15 terms are exact (< 2^53); reduce with fmod. numpy's int64 matmul has no BLAS, so it was the
  bottleneck (15 of 17 s in a profile).
* Measured (single flattening, same inputs, old int64 code -> new): d=9 ((9),(9),(9)) 16 s -> 0.2 s;
  d=8 ((4,3,1),(3,3,1,1),(3,2,2,1)) 130 s / 67 s -> 0.4 s; d=7 ((4,2),(3,2,1),(3,1,1,1)) 12-13 s -> 0.2-0.4 s.
  Whole sweeps: C^4 d=6 29 min -> 83 s with identical ranks (345 fields vs hwv4_d6.log; d=4, d=5 identical too);
  C^3 d=5 positive control finds exactly the three known separators (3/6, 12/15, 12/15 and V1 15/21).
* sweep_fast.py, differences from sweep_hwv.py: exactly g fillings whose functionals are independent (checked on a
